The radical trajectory of Radical Orthodoxy remains constrained by its persistent commitment to a particularist orthodoxy, a fidelity that ultimately undermines its own subversive potential. To be more radical, this theological project must abandon its nostalgic recuperation of a unified Christian metaphysics and instead embrace a pluralistic, post-confessional vision. Such a re-envisioning entails a willingness to let go of dogmatic certainty in favour of an open-ended, dialogical engagement with secular philosophy and other religious traditions. Only by relinquishing its orthodox anchor can this movement secure a genuinely transformative critique of modernity, one no longer tethered to the very metaphysical exclusivity it seeks to overcome. The future of its protest lies in a heresy more faithful to its radical impulse.
