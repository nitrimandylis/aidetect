Maoism fundamentally reshaped the Confucian vision of the Chinese self by displacing the hierarchical, familial‑oriented model with a collective, revolutionary identity rooted in class struggle and service to the proletariat. While Confucianism emphasized self‑cultivation through filial piety, ritual propriety, and moral harmony within a stable social order, Maoist ideology redirected personal development toward political consciousness, mass mobilization, and the subordination of individual aspirations to the goals of the Party and the nation. Consequently, the self became less a private moral agent and more a public instrument of ideological transformation, illustrating Maoism’s extensive redefinition, though traces of Confucian self‑discipline persisted in the rhetoric of self‑sacrifice and moral dedication in contemporary China.
