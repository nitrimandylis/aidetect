Overall, the design of Federation Square demonstrates a nuanced compromise between ambitious urban vision and practical governance, rendering it a successful case study in integrated public space creation. Its layered programmatic offerings, emphasis on pedestrian connectivity, and use of sustainable materials have fostered both daily usage and cultural relevance, fulfilling key criteria of successful urban design. Yet, shortcomings such as limited adaptability to evolving community needs, insufficient visual identity at street level, and initial cost overruns reveal areas where the design fell short of expectations. Consequently, while Federation Square stands as a landmark achievement in Melbourne’s urban landscape, its intended success remains partially qualified by these functional and aesthetic limitations.
