Recent sociopolitical shifts have intensified visibility of LGBTQ+ individuals, yet concomitant reports of harassment based on sexual orientation persist across educational, occupational, and digital environments. This extended essay investigates how such harassment influences the psychological wellbeing of affected persons, focusing on anxiety, depression, self‑esteem, and identity development. By synthesizing quantitative survey data with qualitative interview excerpts, the study seeks to elucidate the mechanisms through which sustained victimization translates into maladaptive cognitive appraisals and emotional dysregulation. The analysis will also consider mediating factors such as social support, coping strategies, and institutional policies, thereby contributing to a nuanced understanding of the psychosocial ramifications of homophobic aggression in contemporary societies worldwide.
