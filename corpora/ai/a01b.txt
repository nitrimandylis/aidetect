Austen consistently employs exterior space in *Emma* as a realm of candid social revelation, where the rigid formalities of Highbury’s interiors dissolve to expose genuine character. The strawberry-picking expedition at Donwell Abbey exemplifies this, as the unstructured garden setting fosters a temporary relaxation of class restraint, permitting Mrs. Elton’s vulgar presumption to surface with unchecked clarity. Similarly, the Box Hill excursion transforms the landscape into an arena of unchecked wit, where Emma’s cruel jest to Miss Bates occurs not within a drawing-room’s polite surveillance, but under the open sky, stripping away social artifice to reveal raw, unmediated impulse. Austen thus frames the outdoors not as a benign retreat, but as a crucible where authentic, and often uncomfortable, human nature is laid bare.
