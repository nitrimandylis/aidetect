The German V-weapon program, comprising the V-1 flying bomb and the V-2 ballistic missile, represented a technologically ambitious yet ultimately failed attempt to reverse the course of the Second World War through strategic bombardment. While these weapons inflicted considerable civilian casualties and psychological terror upon London and Antwerp, their operational impact remained far below the Nazi leadership’s expectations of decisively crippling Allied morale and logistics. This investigation will critically evaluate the multifaceted reasons behind the program’s failure, examining the strategic misdirection of resources away from more effective air-defense systems, the persistent technical limitations that undermined accuracy and reliability, and the concerted Allied countermeasures that successfully mitigated the threat. By analyzing these factors, the essay will demonstrate that the V-weapon program was a strategic miscalculation that accelerated, rather than prevented, Germany’s defeat.
