The divergence between TFP and conventional Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is most acute in their conceptualization of the therapeutic relationship and the locus of pathology. Where CBT identifies maladaptive cognitions as the primary target for structured intervention, TFP posits that the patient’s core pathology is an unintegrated identity diffusion, which inevitably manifests within the dyad. Consequently, the TFP therapist actively invites and interprets the transference, utilizing the “here-and-now” emotional activation as the principal mechanism of change. This represents a fundamental paradigmatic shift: CBT aims to equip the patient with skills to manage symptoms externally, whereas TFP strives for structural personality integration through the lived, affectively charged experience of the therapeutic relationship itself.
