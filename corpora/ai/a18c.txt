The historiographical debate surrounding the 1932–33 Ukrainian famine, or Holodomor, reveals that the application of the term “genocide” has evolved beyond a purely legal or academic classification into a powerful political instrument. While the Soviet Union rigorously suppressed any narrative suggesting the famine was a deliberate act of extermination, the Ukrainian diaspora subsequently wielded the genocide label to affirm a distinct national identity and condemn Soviet imperialism. In contemporary geopolitics, this designation serves a dual purpose: it functions as a moral reckoning with historical trauma for Ukraine, while simultaneously acting as a strategic lever against modern Russian influence. Ultimately, the persistent contestation over this terminology demonstrates that historical truth is not merely an objective record, but a malleable tool mobilized to shape national consciousness and international legitimacy.
