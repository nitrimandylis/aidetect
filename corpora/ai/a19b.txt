Fukuyama’s thesis therefore appears premature primarily because its philosophical foundation—the Hegelian struggle for recognition—fails to account for the persistence of non-liberal forms of identity politics that violently reject Western universalism. While he anticipated the obsolescence of ideology following the Soviet collapse, events such as the rise of radical Islamism, ethnic nationalism in the Balkans, and the resurgence of authoritarian capitalism in China demonstrate that historical contestation merely shifted its axis rather than reaching a terminus. Moreover, Fukuyama underestimated the internal contradictions within liberal democracy itself, including rising populism, economic inequality, and institutional erosion, which suggest that liberal democratic consensus is not a stable endpoint but a contingent and reversible equilibrium.
