Drawing upon the psychodynamic tradition yet distinguished by its structured, manualized approach for severe personality disorders, Transference Focused Psychotherapy (TFP), developed by Otto Kernberg, offers a distinct lens through which to understand and treat psychological distress. This extended essay undertakes a comparative evaluation of TFP against another prominent therapeutic modality, examining their divergent theoretical underpinnings, core mechanisms of change, and empirical support. By analyzing their respective conceptualizations of pathology, particularly regarding identity diffusion and relational patterns, this investigation seeks to illuminate the unique contributions and potential limitations of each framework, ultimately assessing their applicability and efficacy in contemporary clinical practice.
