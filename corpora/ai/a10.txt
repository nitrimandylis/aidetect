Hate crimes and harassment targeting LGBT youth constitute a distinct and pervasive form of victimisation, defined not merely by criminal acts such as assault or vandalism but by the perpetrator’s bias against the victim’s actual or perceived sexual orientation or gender identity. This bias motivation transforms an ordinary offence into a symbolic attack intended to intimidate the entire community. Prevalence studies, while hampered by chronic underreporting, consistently reveal alarming rates of exposure: national surveys in the United States indicate that a majority of LGBT students experience verbal harassment at school, with significant minorities facing physical assault based on their identity. Cyberbullying has further expanded the arena for such abuse, creating environments of relentless persecution that profoundly impair the psychological and educational development of these young people.
