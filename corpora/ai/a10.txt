In order to examine the experiences of LGBT young people, it is first necessary to define the terms under discussion, since hate crime and harassment are related but distinct phenomena. A hate crime is generally understood as a criminal offence, such as assault or the destruction of property, that is motivated wholly or in part by hostility towards the victim's actual or perceived sexual orientation or gender identity, whereas harassment refers to a broader pattern of unwanted behaviour, including verbal abuse, intimidation and social exclusion, which need not in itself constitute a crime. Establishing the prevalence of such victimisation is complicated by significant underreporting, as many young people are reluctant to disclose incidents to authorities for fear of exposure or further hostility. Nevertheless, survey research consistently indicates that LGBT youth experience these forms of aggression at markedly higher rates than their heterosexual and cisgender peers, most frequently within school environments, where the effects on wellbeing can be considerable.
