Ultimately, the failure of the German V-weapon program was not a result of technological inadequacy but of strategic miscalculation. While the V-1 and V-2 represented revolutionary advances in rocketry and pilotless aircraft, their employment as indiscriminate terror weapons against civilian populations squandered their limited military potential. The Allies’ effective countermeasures, including aerial interdiction, deception campaigns, and the rapid overrunning of launch sites, further mitigated their impact. Moreover, the immense financial and industrial resources diverted into these programs came at the direct expense of conventional air defense and fighter production, which were more urgently needed by 1944. Consequently, the program hastened Germany’s collapse without altering the strategic balance, rendering it a costly and ultimately counterproductive venture.
