In conclusion, the evidence compiled for this essay indicates that intensive, early, and individualized Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) can produce measurable gains in communicative, social, and adaptive functioning for many children diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder, though the degree of recovery remains heterogeneous and contingent upon factors such as intensity, onset, family involvement, and complementary therapeutic supports. While controlled studies and meta‑analyses demonstrate statistically significant effect sizes, methodological limitations — particularly reliance on short‑term outcomes and variability in operational definitions of ‘recovery’ — caution against overgeneralising these results. Consequently, policy recommendations emphasise universal screening, timely access to high‑quality ABA services, and ongoing monitoring to optimise developmental trajectories without inflating expectations of complete symptom remission.
