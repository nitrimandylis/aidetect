Analyzing the transformation of the Confucian “self” under Maoist ideology reveals a decisive rupture: where Confucianism situates identity within hierarchical relational networks anchored by virtue and ritual, Maoism replaces filial and moral obligations with the collective mission of the proletarian revolution, positioning the individual as a malleable agent of historical change. This redefinition foregrounds the “mass line” and self‑criticism as mechanisms for cultivating a politically conscious subjectivity, thereby dissolving traditional ego‑centric autonomy in favor of a socially engineered collectivism. Consequently, the Maoist self is constructed through continual ideological learning and praxis, emphasizing sacrifice to the party and the nation over personal cultivation, thereby reshaping Confucian conceptions of moral interiority.
