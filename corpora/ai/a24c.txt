The analysis demonstrates that the once‑robust bilingualism in rural Paraguayan communities is eroding under the combined pressures of urban migration, digital media homogenization, and shifting socioeconomic hierarchies, leaving linguistic isolation as an unintended byproduct of modernization; while this transition reflects broader patterns of language attrition, it also reveals moments of resilience where isolated speakers deliberately preserve heritage forms as cultural anchors. Consequently, the study underscores the urgent need for community‑driven revitalization projects that leverage local knowledge and reproduce everyday contexts of language use, suggesting that policy interventions must prioritize grassroots participation over top‑down educational mandates to counteract isolation and sustain the region’s linguistic diversity before it slips irretrievably into silence.
