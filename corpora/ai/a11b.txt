The emergence of football in San José during the early twentieth century cannot be separated from the ideological tensions reshaping Costa Rican society. Initially introduced through British and German expatriate communities, and later adopted by students returning from Europe, the sport quickly became a contested cultural space. Elite institutions such as the Colegio de San Luis Gonzaga promoted football as a marker of modernity and European sophistication, while liberal and anarcho-syndicalist circles viewed the game as a vehicle for worker organisation and popular solidarity. Club rivalries in the capital frequently mirrored divisions between traditional oligarchic families and reformist political movements, with matches occasionally serving as sites of protest against economic inequality.
