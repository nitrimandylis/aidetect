The V-weapon program's failure stemmed fundamentally from a misallocation of scarce resources toward strategically irrelevant objectives. While the V-2 represented a remarkable technological achievement—the world's first ballistic missile—it consumed aluminium, fuel, and skilled labour desperately needed for conventional aircraft production, with estimates suggesting each rocket cost as much as several fighter planes. Militarily, the weapons lacked the accuracy to strike anything smaller than a city, rendering them terror instruments rather than decisive war-winning tools, and their approximately 9,000 deaths in Britain paled against the resources expended. Furthermore, the program's reliance on Mittelwerk's slave labour damaged German war production through sabotage and deliberate over-engineering. Ultimately, Hitler's obsession with retaliation weapons diverted German industry from defensive priorities at precisely the moment Allied air superiority demanded fighter production, illustrating how technological sophistication without strategic coherence hastened rather than delayed defeat.
