The most significant continuity in Japanese policy was the persistent subordination of Jewish welfare to imperial strategic objectives. Initially, the “Fugu Plan” of 1938 envisioned leveraging purported Jewish financial and political influence to develop Manchukuo and secure American goodwill, mirroring a pragmatic, utilitarian approach. This instrumentalization continued even after the plan’s abandonment, as the 1942 decision to confine stateless refugees to the Shanghai Ghetto was not driven by ideological antisemitism akin to their German ally, but by a perceived security necessity under pressure from Nazi Germany. Thus, the shift from encouragement to containment represents a change in tactic, not a fundamental alteration of the principle that Jewish lives were ultimately expendable pawns in Japan’s broader geopolitical calculus.
