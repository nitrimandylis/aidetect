The classical guitar’s ascent from a salon instrument to a respected concert voice owes much to Andrés Segovia, whose career was built upon an extensive repertoire largely transcribed from other instruments. This essay investigates the extent to which Segovia’s own recorded performances and his published transcriptions can be considered definitive interpretative models for the modern guitarist. By examining specific examples, such as his transcription of J.S. Bach’s Chaconne and his recordings of works by Albéniz and Granados, this analysis will assess the balance between textual fidelity, Romantic performance practice, and personal artistic licence. Ultimately, it evaluates whether Segovia’s legacy lies in an authoritative tradition or a singular, historically situated aesthetic.
