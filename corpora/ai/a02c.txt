Consequently, the examination of attitudes toward human spirituality in Goethe’s Faust and Marlowe’s Doctor Faustus reveals a nuanced trajectory from medieval damnation to modern existential yearning, illustrating how each author negotiates the tension between knowledge and redemption. Goethe’s Faust emerges as a questing soul whose spiritual ascent is mediated through Romantic individualism, suggesting that transcendence lies in perpetual striving rather than doctrinal adherence. In contrast, Marlowe’s Faustus embodies the crisis of a Renaissance intellect whose bargain with demonic forces underscores a fatalistic hubris that denies divine grace. Collectively, the texts underscore spirituality’s mutable nature, emphasizing that human aspiration can both illuminate and endanger the soul’s ultimate destiny.
