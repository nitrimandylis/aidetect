Tolkien’s portrayal of female agency unfolds through nuanced subversions of patriarchal tropes, where Arwen’s choice of mortality, Galadriel’s strategic wisdom, and Éowyn’s martial valor collectively redefine femininity within Middle‑earth; these characters wield influence not merely through lineage or beauty but via deliberate agency, linguistic empowerment, and moral authority, thereby foregrounding an emergent feminist discourse that challenges the hegemonic male epic tradition while preserving the mythic integrity of the narrative. Moreover, the interplay of hearth‑centered domestic spaces with public realms allows Tolkien to juxtapose nurturing domestic authority against battlefield heroism, suggesting that true power often emanates from the quiet resilience of women whose contributions sustain the fellowship’s moral compass and the broader mythos.
