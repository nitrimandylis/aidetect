In Centro Oberhausen, restaurants operate under monopolistic competition, where numerous firms sell differentiated meals yet face highly elastic demand due to close substitutes. Because consumers can easily switch between cafés, fast-food outlets, and sit-down restaurants, individual firms possess limited price-setting power. Field observations reveal that many restaurants cluster their prices within narrow bands, suggesting tacit price rigidity: raising prices risks significant loss of customers, while undercutting rivals may trigger retaliation and erode profits. Consequently, firms compete primarily through non-price strategies, including portion size, ambience, promotions, and meal deals, which shift demand rather than engaging in destructive price wars. Nonetheless, promotional discounts demonstrate that implicit price competition persists, particularly during off-peak hours when marginal customers are most price-sensitive.
