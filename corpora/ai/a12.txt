The concentration of restaurants in the Centro district of Oberhausen provides a clear illustration of price competition under monopolistic competition, a market structure in which numerous firms sell products that are similar but differentiated. Because each establishment offers a slightly distinct menu, atmosphere and location, it retains a limited degree of market power and faces a demand curve that slopes downward rather than being perfectly elastic. At the same time, the sheer number of close substitutes within walking distance means that any restaurant which raises its prices substantially above those of its rivals risks losing a large share of its customers to nearby competitors. The survey data, which show that most diners expect to pay within a fairly narrow range and that prices across the restaurants cluster closely together, are consistent with this analysis. Firms therefore tend to compete through quality and differentiation rather than through aggressive price cutting, since sustained undercutting would be quickly matched and would erode profits for all.
