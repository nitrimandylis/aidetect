Francis Fukuyama’s 1989 essay “The End of History?” provocatively argued that the collapse of Soviet communism signaled not merely the end of the Cold War, but the terminal point of humanity’s ideological evolution, with Western liberal democracy emerging as the final form of government. This Extended Essay critically evaluates the extent to which Fukuyama’s thesis was premature by examining subsequent geopolitical developments that challenge his linear conception of historical progress. The investigation focuses on three principal counterpoints: the resurgence of ethno-nationalist conflicts in the Balkans during the 1990s, the rise of transnational Islamist terrorism culminating in the September 11 attacks, and the increasing authoritarian resilience demonstrated by states such as China and Russia.
