The evidence gathered from the Mhlumeni community ultimately demonstrates that achieving sustainable land use in rural post-apartheid South Africa requires a fundamental integration of land restitution with targeted agricultural development. While the reversal of historical dispossession provides essential social justice and a foundation for security, it remains insufficient without robust state support for infrastructure, market access, and modern farming techniques. The community's persistent challenges, including soil degradation and economic marginalization, underscore the legacy of apartheid spatial planning and the failure of neoliberal agricultural policies. Therefore, true sustainability is contingent upon a holistic, community-centred approach that prioritizes ecological regeneration and economic empowerment, moving beyond mere land transfer to foster resilient and self-sufficient rural livelihoods.
