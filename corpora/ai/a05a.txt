From the moment Gandalf first urges Frodo to bear the One Ring, Tolkien foregrounds a subtle yet powerful reshaping of gender dynamics, wherein the traditionally marginal feminine voice expands into a force of agency and moral clarity. Through characters such as Galadriel, Arwen, and even the seemingly peripheral Éowyn, the narrative interrogates the boundaries of power, courage, and destiny, presenting them not merely as auxiliary figures but as custodians of wisdom and destiny. This essay argues that Tolkien’s depiction of the “feminine” operates on both literal and symbolic levels, revealing an underlying empowerment that challenges patriarchal structures within the mythic framework, and explores how these portrayals intersect with contemporary feminist readings.
