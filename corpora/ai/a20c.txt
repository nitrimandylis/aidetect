In sum, the investigated literature confirms that homophobic harassment exerts profound psychological repercussions on LGBTQ+ individuals, fostering anxiety, depressive symptoms, and diminished self‑esteem while simultaneously impairing interpersonal functioning and academic or occupational performance. These effects are amplified when victimization persists across multiple contexts and is compounded by institutional indifference or overt hostility. Nevertheless, protective factors such as supportive peer networks, affirmative counseling, and policy interventions emerge as mitigating agents capable of attenuating distress and promoting resilience. Consequently, the essay underscores the urgency for comprehensive educational reforms and mental‑health resources that directly address harassment dynamics to safeguard the well‑being of sexual minorities. Future research should further delineate longitudinal pathways to inform evidence‑based interventions.
