A comparative analysis of Segovia's recordings with his published transcriptions reveals a consistent tension between editorial fidelity and performative freedom. In his rendition of Tárrega's Recuerdos de la Alhambra, Segovia's tremolo maintains the notated rhythmic structure, yet his rubato at phrase cadences diverges markedly from the score's metronomic implications, suggesting that he treated the transcription as a framework rather than a prescription. Similarly, his recording of the Bach Chaconne demonstrates voicing decisions—emphasising contrapuntal lines through dynamic shading—that remain unmarked in the printed text. These discrepancies indicate that Segovia's artistry depended substantially on unnotated interpretive choices, rendering his transcriptions incomplete representations of his performances. Consequently, the recordings preserve nuances of phrasing, timbre, and temporal flexibility that the transcriptions alone cannot convey, underscoring the limitations of notation in capturing the full expressive reality of his interpretations.
