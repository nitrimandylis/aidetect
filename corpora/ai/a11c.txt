In conclusion, the early development of football in San José cannot be understood apart from the social and political tensions that shaped Costa Rica at the turn of the twentieth century. Introduced by foreign elites and returning students, the sport quickly became an arena in which class divisions, regional rivalries, and debates over national identity were contested. The formation of clubs reflected both the aspirations of the emerging middle classes and the exclusionary practices of the elite, while matches between rival teams often mirrored broader disputes between competing political and social factions. Ultimately, football in San José served not merely as recreation but as a symbolic battleground. Its evolution demonstrates how sport can embody and reproduce societal conflicts, offering historians valuable insight into the complexities of Costa Rican nation-building during this transformative period.
