In conclusion, Picasso’s incorporation of the Mbangu mask into *Les Demoiselles d’Avignon* cannot be judged as a purely neutral artistic synthesis, but nor should it be condemned as outright theft without context. While his engagement undeniably decontextualized a sacred object, reducing its ritualistic significance to a formal device for subverting Western pictorial conventions, it simultaneously exposed European audiences to African aesthetic systems and challenged entrenched colonial hierarchies of taste. The act was thus deeply ambiguous: an appropriation that both erased original meaning and created a powerful, if problematic, visual dialogue. Ultimately, Picasso’s borrowing was neither wholly appropriate nor entirely indefensible, but rather a complex, historically embedded act that underscores the ethical limits of artistic innovation when encountering the cultural property of another society.
