The intensity of price competition among sit-down restaurants in Centro Oberhausen reflects the theoretical expectations of monopolistic competition, albeit moderated by non-price differentiation. With over twenty eateries offering substitutable meals, firms face highly elastic demand curves, meaning a unilateral price increase would likely cause significant customer migration to close alternatives. However, observed menu prices for comparable dishes, such as pasta or burgers, varied by only 10–15%, suggesting that aggressive undercutting is avoided. Restaurants instead compete through perceived quality, cuisine type, ambience, and location within the mall, allowing them to maintain a degree of pricing power despite numerous rivals. Consequently, while price is a factor, it functions more as a competitive boundary than an active weapon, confirming that monopolistically competitive firms prioritise product differentiation to preserve mark-ups and avoid mutually destructive price wars in a confined retail environment.
