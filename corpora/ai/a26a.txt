As one of Australia’s most ambitious and contentious public projects, Melbourne’s Federation Square provides a compelling case study for evaluating contemporary urban design. Opened in 2002, its deconstructivist architecture by Lab Architecture Studio was intended to create a new civic heart for the city, linking the central business district to the Yarra River. However, since its completion, the square has provoked fierce debate among architects, planners, and the public, with opinions ranging from celebrated icon to dysfunctional eyesore. This essay investigates the extent of that success by assessing the project against its original civic, social, and aesthetic objectives. Through an analysis of public engagement, architectural critique, and functional performance, it becomes possible to move beyond subjective taste toward a balanced evaluation of Federation Square’s legacy.
