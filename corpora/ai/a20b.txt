The analysis reveals a statistically significant correlation between the frequency of experienced homosexual harassment and elevated psychological distress among the sample. Participants reporting higher instances of victimisation, including verbal slurs and social exclusion, consistently scored lower on measures of self-esteem and higher on indicators of anxiety and depression. This pattern aligns with the Minority Stress Model, which posits that chronic, prejudice-related stressors accumulate to create a unique psychological burden. Moreover, qualitative data underscored the internalisation of this harassment, as respondents described persistent hypervigilance in social settings and a resulting erosion of their sense of safety. These findings suggest the damage extends beyond immediate emotional harm, potentially altering long-term cognitive schemas related to identity and belonging.
