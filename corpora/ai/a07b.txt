The analysis reveals that the Mhlumeni community demonstrates a mixed pattern of land‑use practices, where traditional communal grazing coexists with emerging conservation initiatives supported by municipal schemes. Statistical comparison of household survey data shows a significant decline in shifting cultivation plots — down 27 % since 2015 — while area under agroforestry increased by 42 %, correlating with participation in the Department of Agriculture’s permaculture workshops. Regression models indicate that access to water infrastructure and off‑farm employment are the strongest predictors of sustainable adoption, explaining 68 % of variance in land‑management decisions. Moreover, cultural values of stewardship mediate the relationship between land tenure security and ecological outcomes, suggesting that policy interventions must integrate socio‑economic empowerment with land‑rights reforms.
