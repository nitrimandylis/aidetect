Ultimately, Derrida’s deconstruction exposes the internal contradictions that destabilise any claim to absolute cultural truth, revealing meaning as perpetually deferred rather than fixed. Consequently, the practical application of postmodern principles to multiculturalism often falters, for its emphasis on radical relativism undermines the very possibility of shared ethical standards necessary for genuine coexistence. Instead of fostering dialogue, this approach can paradoxically entrench cultural essentialism by demanding uncritical acceptance of all practices, thereby foreclosing the rigorous critique Derrida himself advocated. The failure lies not in deconstruction’s analytical power, but in its reduction to a simplistic, relativistic slogan that mistakes the acknowledgment of difference for a substantive political or ethical programme, leaving multiculturalism theoretically rich yet practically incapable of adjudicating conflict.
