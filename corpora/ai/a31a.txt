In the vibrant urban district of Centro, Oberhausen, the restaurant industry exemplifies a monopolistically competitive market where numerous establishments offer differentiated dining experiences while competing for consumer preference. This essay examines how price competition shapes firm behavior and market outcomes in such an environment, focusing on the strategic pricing decisions of selected eateries. By analysing price variations, menu assortments, and service attributes, the study seeks to identify the extent to which competitive pressures influence price elasticity, market entry barriers, and consumer welfare. The investigation situates these dynamics within broader economic theory, aiming to contribute empirical insight into the practical manifestations of monopolistic competition in a contemporary German metropolitan context.
