Despite variations in regional policy frameworks, this investigation finds that geographic location exerts a measurable but not monolithic influence on the educational attainment of Indigenous secondary students across Queensland and Victoria, with remoteness amplifying systemic disadvantages in access to resources, teacher retention, and culturally responsive curricula. While urban Indigenous students benefit from greater institutional diversity and support networks, their outcomes remain constrained by socioeconomic segregation and disengagement from mainstream pedagogy. Ultimately, location functions not as an isolated variable but as a spatial proxy for the uneven distribution of structural opportunity, underscoring the need for place-sensitive policy that addresses underlying inequities in both metropolitan and regional settings.
