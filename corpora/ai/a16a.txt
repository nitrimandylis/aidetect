Jacques Derrida’s deconstruction offers a critical lens for exposing the inherent contradictions within the pragmatic applications of postmodern theory to multicultural societies, revealing how ostensibly inclusive policies often mask persistent power asymmetries. By interrogating the binary oppositions that undergird notions of tolerance, identity, and cultural relativism, Derrida demonstrates that the so‑called postmodern embrace of plurality frequently collapses into a superficial moral relativism that fails to address structural inequities. This introduction situates the essay’s inquiry at the intersection of philosophical critique and sociopolitical analysis, arguing that the fallacies of practical postmodernism — its reliance on linguistic indeterminacy and stylistic abstraction — undermine its capacity to foster genuine intercultural dialogue and equitable governance in pluralistic contexts.
