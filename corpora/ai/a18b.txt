The classification of the 1932–33 Ukrainian famine as genocide has itself become a potent political instrument, wielded to shape national identity and geopolitical narratives. Within Ukraine, the term “Holodomor” functions as a cornerstone of post-Soviet state-building, fostering collective memory and legitimizing independence by framing the famine as a deliberate assault on the Ukrainian nation. Internationally, particularly following Russia’s 2022 invasion, this designation has been strategically employed by Western governments to delegitimize Russian historical narratives and galvanize support. By recognizing the famine as genocide, these actors directly challenge the Kremlin’s portrayal of the event as a tragic but non-targeted Soviet disaster, thereby weaponizing historical memory to contest contemporary political influence and underscore the continuity of Russian imperial aggression.
