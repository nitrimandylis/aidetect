The framing of the 1932–33 Ukrainian famine, or Holodomor, as an act of genocide remains one of the most contested historical debates precisely because its classification carries profound political weight. While Soviet historiography long suppressed the famine or dismissed it as a tragic consequence of collectivization, Ukrainian diaspora communities and, later, the independent Ukrainian state advanced a narrative of deliberate, targeted destruction. This essay will examine how the term “genocide” has been deployed not merely as a legal or historical descriptor but as a strategic instrument, used by various actors to legitimize national identity, delegitimize Soviet and post-Soviet Russian narratives, and galvanize international political support for Ukraine.
