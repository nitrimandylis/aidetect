In the turbulent mid‑twentieth century, Mao Zedong’s revolutionary ideology intersected with the enduring Confucian tradition that had long articulated a relational understanding of the self, shaping contemporary debates about identity and agency. This thesis investigates the paradoxical process whereby Maoist doctrine, with its emphasis on collective praxis and class struggle, both appropriated and reconfigured the Confucian notion of “self” as an agent situated within moral and social hierarchies. By examining key texts, political campaigns, and literary reflections, the essay seeks to determine whether Maoism transformed the classical Confucian self‑conception into a more instrumental, politically charged construct, thereby revealing a complex dialogue between indigenous philosophical heritage and centralized authority.
