Ultimately, Francis Fukuyama’s thesis in *The End of History?* was indeed premature, though not entirely without merit as a reflection of the immediate post-Cold War zeitgeist. The resurgence of ethnic nationalism in the Balkans, the rise of religious fundamentalism culminating in 9/11, and the authoritarian resilience of China fundamentally challenged the notion of an inevitable, global convergence towards liberal democracy. While Fukuyama correctly identified the exhaustion of grand ideological alternatives to liberalism in 1989, he underestimated the enduring power of culture and identity as drivers of conflict. Consequently, history did not end; it merely entered a more fragmented and complex phase, proving that the universal triumph of liberal democratic ideals was a hopeful aspiration rather than a definitive reality.
