Before the maximum braking force of a bicycle can be calculated, the position of the combined centre of mass of the rider and bicycle must be established, since this determines how the normal forces on the two wheels redistribute during deceleration. The centre of mass was located both horizontally and vertically using suspension and balance methods: the rider assumed a typical braking posture while scales beneath each wheel recorded the weight distribution, from which the horizontal distance of the centre of mass relative to the front wheel's contact point was found by taking moments. The vertical height was estimated by suspending the rider–bicycle system and applying the principle of moments about a chosen pivot.
