Before the conditions for maximum braking on a bicycle can be analysed, it is necessary to determine the position of the combined centre of mass of the rider and machine, since this quantity governs how weight is transferred between the wheels during deceleration. The centre of mass may be treated as the single point at which the total weight of the system can be considered to act, and its location relative to the two points of contact with the ground determines the limiting deceleration before the rear wheel lifts. In order to establish this position experimentally, the horizontal coordinate can be found by treating the bicycle as a rigid body supported at the wheels and applying the principle of moments to the measured reaction forces, while the vertical coordinate requires a separate procedure in which the machine is inclined and the resulting change in the reactions is recorded. Only once both coordinates are known can the subsequent dynamic analysis proceed.
