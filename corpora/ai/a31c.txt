In conclusion, the restaurant sector within Centro, Oberhausen, provides a compelling illustration of how price competition operates under the theoretical framework of monopolistic competition. The analysis revealed that while individual eateries retain some pricing autonomy through product differentiation—such as cuisine type, brand identity, and service quality—the dense concentration of rivals inevitably constrains this power. Price reductions by one establishment triggered observable competitive responses, yet the persistent differentiation prevented a pure price war, as consumer loyalty to specific dining concepts buffered extreme elasticity. Ultimately, the findings affirm that non-price competition, particularly location and unique menu offerings, is often more decisive than marginal price changes in determining long-term viability, demonstrating the intricate balance between market power and competitive interdependence characteristic of monopolistic structures.
