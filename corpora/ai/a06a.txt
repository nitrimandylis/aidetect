The educational outcomes of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander secondary students in Australia remain a critical focal point for policy and research, yet the nuanced role of geographic location often obscures the diverse realities within this demographic. This essay investigates the extent to which residing in the remote regions of Queensland, as opposed to the more urbanized landscapes of Victoria, influences the educational attainment of Indigenous secondary students. By juxtaposing these two states, this study seeks to move beyond broad national averages, which frequently mask significant regional disparities in access to resources, cultural continuity, and institutional support. The central question is whether location acts as a primary determinant of academic success or as a variable interwoven with socioeconomic and historical factors.
