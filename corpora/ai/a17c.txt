In evaluating the collapse of the Oslo‑derived peace talks between 2000 and 2001, it becomes evident that while structural asymmetries and external shocks played decisive roles, Yasser Arafat’s political calculus significantly contributed to the impasse; his reluctance to endorse a comprehensive security arrangement, his insistence on the right of return, and his failure to consolidate a unified Palestinian negotiating front undermined trust and emboldened Israeli hardliners, thereby accelerating the breakdown of dialogue; yet attributing sole responsibility to Arafat oversimplifies a complex interplay of leadership miscalculations, asymmetrical power dynamics, and contextual pressures, suggesting that accountability must be shared across both parties rather than pinned exclusively on one figure in the broader historiographical debate.
