Circadian rhythms are endogenous, roughly 24-hour cycles in physiological and behavioural processes, driven by internal biological clocks that persist even in the absence of external time cues. In mammals, the master pacemaker resides in the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) of the hypothalamus, which synchronises peripheral oscillators throughout the body and is entrained primarily by light detected in the retina. At the molecular level, these rhythms arise from transcription-translation feedback loops involving clock genes such as Period and Cryptochrome. The golden hamster (Mesocricetus auratus) is a classic model organism in chronobiology because its wheel-running activity exhibits an exceptionally precise and stable circadian period, close to 24 hours, allowing small changes in period or phase to be measured reliably.
