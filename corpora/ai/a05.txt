Circadian rhythms are endogenous biological cycles of approximately twenty-four hours that regulate a wide range of physiological and behavioural processes, from the sleep-wake cycle and body temperature to patterns of hormone secretion and locomotor activity. Although these rhythms persist even under constant conditions, demonstrating that they are generated internally rather than imposed by the environment, they are normally entrained to external cues, of which the daily cycle of light and darkness is the most important. In mammals the principal internal clock is located in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus, which receives information about ambient light and coordinates the timing of subordinate oscillators throughout the body. Because this timing mechanism can be reset by appropriately timed stimuli, the study of how light pulses shift the phase of an organism's activity provides a valuable means of investigating the underlying properties of the clock, and it is this sensitivity that the present experiment on hamsters seeks to examine.
