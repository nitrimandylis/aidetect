In post‑apartheid South Africa, the transformation of land tenure and agricultural practices in rural communities has become a critical arena for examining sustainability, yet scholarly attention often overlooks how historic marginalisation continues to shape contemporary land use. This extended essay investigates the case of the Mhlumeni community in KwaZulu‑Natal, exploring how local households negotiate communal stewardship, market pressures, and conservation policies while adapting to environmental constraints. By integrating qualitative interviews with quantitative assessments of soil health and water availability, the study seeks to answer how sustainable land use can simultaneously address socio‑economic equity and ecological resilience. Insights from this analysis may inform broader policy frameworks aimed at equitable resource governance across similar rural contexts." Now count words.
