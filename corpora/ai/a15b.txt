Kant’s classification of the suicide maxim as a perfect duty to oneself hinges on the logical impossibility of willing its universalisation, yet the precise nature of this contradiction in conception warrants closer scrutiny. The maxim, “from self-love I make it my principle to shorten my life when its continued duration threatens more ill than it promises agreeableness,” relies on the same feeling—self-love—to both preserve and destroy life. Kant argues that a nature whose law impelled self-love to terminate life would contradict itself and therefore subsist as no nature at all. However, this argument conflates a contradiction in the functional application of a feeling with a logical contradiction in the concept of nature.
