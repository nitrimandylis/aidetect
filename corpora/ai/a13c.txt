The recordings and transcriptions of Andrés Segovia collectively affirm that his influence on the classical guitar was foundational rather than merely popularizing, establishing a performance practice that prioritised tonal variety, expressive rubato, and a pianistic approach to polyphony. While his interpretative choices sometimes deviated from strict historical fidelity, particularly in Baroque repertoire, these deviations were instrumental in elevating the guitar’s status as a concert instrument capable of nuanced artistic expression. His transcriptions, though occasionally criticised for their romanticised alterations, systematically expanded the instrument’s limited canon and demonstrated its capacity to convey complex musical structures. Ultimately, Segovia’s recorded legacy functions as both a pedagogical blueprint and a historical artefact, revealing a deliberate strategy to legitimise the guitar within Western art music through performance and repertoire.
