The introduction of football to San José at the dawn of the twentieth century was far more than a sporting import; it was a cultural catalyst that illuminated the deep fissures of Costa Rican society. As the nation’s liberal elites sought to project an image of modernity and European cosmopolitanism, they embraced the sport as a symbol of progress, codifying rules and forming exclusive clubs that mirrored their own hierarchical social order. Simultaneously, the game was adopted by the growing working classes and artisan communities, who transformed informal matches into sites of popular expression and resistance against aristocratic paternalism. Thus, the early development of football provides a unique lens through which to examine the ongoing social and political disputes over national identity.
