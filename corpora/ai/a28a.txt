The study investigates whether Picasso’s appropriation of the African‑inspired Mbangu mask in Les Demoiselles d’Avignon constitutes cultural borrowing, exoticism, or a legitimizing artistic dialogue. By examining contemporaneous ethnographic sources, Picasso’s own statements, and the mask’s formal and symbolic dimensions within 1906 Parisian artistic circles, the essay assesses how the motif contributes to the work’s formal innovations and its impact on African art perceptions. This analysis raises broader questions about artistic agency, colonial discourse, and ethical responsibility, framing the central research question: to what extent does the incorporation of the Mbangu mask reflect an informed engagement with African cultures or a superficial appropriation that reinforces power asymmetries?"
