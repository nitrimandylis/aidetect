The quantitative data gathered through the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS) reveals a strong, albeit partial, correlation between intensive Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) and the mitigation of core autistic symptoms. While none of the five children in the longitudinal study achieved a complete “recovery”—defined here as the total absence of diagnostic criteria—all demonstrated statistically significant improvements in joint attention and functional communication. For instance, two participants moved from Level 3 to Level 1 severity ratings following thirty hours of weekly one-to-one therapy. However, this analysis suggests that the term “recovery” is misleading, as persistent deficits in social reciprocity and rigid behavioural patterns remained. Consequently, ABA appears to facilitate a form of behavioural compensation rather than a neurological cure, challenging the absolutist language often found in early intervention literature.
