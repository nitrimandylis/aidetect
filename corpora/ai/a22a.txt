The contemporary conceptualisation of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) has shifted from a rare, monolithic condition to a widely recognised spectrum of neurodevelopmental differences, prompting significant debate over its aetiology and, most critically, its treatment. Among the various therapeutic interventions, Applied Behaviour Analysis (ABA) has emerged as the most empirically supported and, simultaneously, the most contentious approach. Proponents of intensive, early ABA-based intervention often cite cases of children achieving an “optimal outcome,” where they lose their formal diagnosis. This contentious notion of “recovery” raises profound questions about the true goals of intervention. This essay will critically investigate the extent to which early intensive behavioural intervention, grounded in the principles of ABA, can lead to a genuine and lasting remission of autistic symptomology.
