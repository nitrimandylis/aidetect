The scholarly debate surrounding the 2000-2001 peace process collapse, particularly regarding Yasser Arafat's culpability, reveals a complex interplay of leadership failures and structural realities. While Arafat's reluctance to accept Barak's final offer at Camp David and his subsequent decision to launch the Second Intifada are frequently cited as critical missteps that shattered diplomatic momentum, attributing sole blame oversimplifies a multifaceted crisis. Factors including Israel's continued settlement expansion, the entrenched distrust stemming from decades of conflict, the impact of U.S. political shifts, and the Palestinian leadership's internal divisions significantly constrained viable negotiation paths. Consequently, most analyses conclude that the failure resulted from systemic breakdowns rather than attributable blame resting exclusively on Arafat, reflecting the inherent difficulty of achieving peace under such protracted and volatile conditions.
