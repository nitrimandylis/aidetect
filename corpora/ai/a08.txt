In this experiment, an ultrasonic motion detector was positioned at the top of a rigid incline to record the position of a uniform cylinder as it rolled from rest down the slope. The detector emitted high-frequency pulses and measured the time delay of their echoes, allowing the data logger to generate position–time and velocity–time graphs at regular intervals. By analysing the gradient of the velocity–time graph, the cylinder's linear acceleration was determined and compared with the theoretical prediction, a = g sin θ / (1 + I/mr²), which for a solid cylinder simplifies to a = (2/3)g sin θ. Systematic discrepancies between the measured and predicted accelerations were attributed to rolling friction, slight misalignment of the incline, and the finite sampling rate of the detector, prompting refinements to the experimental procedure.
