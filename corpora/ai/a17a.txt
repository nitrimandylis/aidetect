Since the Oslo Accords reshaped Middle‑Eastern diplomacy in the 1990s, expectations for a comprehensive peace settlement intensified, yet the turbulent years 2000‑2001 marked a decisive rupture. The violent intifada that erupted following Ariel Sharon’s provocative visit to the Temple Mount, coupled with the collapse of the Camp David II talks, exposed deep mistrust and divergent narratives between Israelis and Palestinians. While scholars debate the relative responsibility of political leadership, many contend that former Palestinian Authority chairman Yitzhak Arafat’s strategic choices — particularly his refusal to abandon the ‘right of return’ and his endorsement of armed resistance — contributed significantly to the breakdown. This essay assesses whether Arafat bore primary culpability for the 2000‑2001 peace process failure.
