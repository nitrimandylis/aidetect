The Geelong Ring Road, completed in stages between 2008 and 2013, was designed to divert through-traffic away from the city centre and suburban streets of Geelong, Victoria. However, large-scale transport infrastructure rarely acts solely as a conduit for vehicles; it can fundamentally reshape patterns of urban growth, land use, accessibility and community structure. This essay investigates the extent to which the construction of the Ring Road has influenced Geelong's urban development, examining changes in traffic distribution, residential expansion in growth corridors such as Armstrong Creek, property values, and the fragmentation of established communities and ecosystems. By analysing transport data, planning documents and demographic trends, this essay argues that while the Ring Road has achieved its primary objective of reducing congestion, it has simultaneously accelerated peri-urban sprawl, raising significant questions about the long-term sustainability of Geelong's urban form and the unintended consequences of road-based infrastructure investment.
