The rapid integration of radio‑frequency identification (RFID) technology into institutional environments has sparked considerable debate regarding its efficacy and ethical implications. In schools, RFID tags are employed to streamline attendance, enhance campus security, and track asset movement, while hospitals utilize them to monitor patient flow, manage equipment inventories, and improve medication safety. Proponents argue that these applications yield operational efficiencies, cost reductions, and heightened safety outcomes. Critics, however, raise concerns about data privacy, surveillance creep, and potential misuse of personal information. This essay will compare the implementation strategies, measurable benefits, and attendant challenges of RFID monitoring in educational and healthcare settings, evaluating whether the purported advantages justify the associated risks.
