The problem of whether a three-by-three field containing two weed parcels can become entirely overgrown reduces to an examination of adjacency conditions within a finite grid. Since a parcel only becomes weed when at least two of its neighbours are already weed, the initial configuration determines the maximum extent of propagation. By considering the rotational symmetry of the arrangements, the number of distinct cases can be reduced substantially, allowing a complete enumeration. This analysis demonstrates that the perimeter of the infected region cannot increase, which provides an elegant invariant and establishes conclusively that total overgrowth is impossible under the stated conditions.
