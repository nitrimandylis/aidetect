This investigation sought to determine whether a 3m x 3m plot containing two initial weed parcels would inevitably become entirely overgrown, and the results suggest a nuanced outcome contingent on species-specific growth dynamics rather than simple spatial expansion. While the experimental data indicated rapid vegetative propagation from the initial clusters, total coverage was not achieved within the observation period, as intraspecific competition for light and soil nutrients created a self-limiting frontier. The mathematical model, based on a reaction-diffusion equation, predicted eventual homogenization only when the weed’s growth rate parameter exceeded a threshold relative to its dispersal coefficient. Consequently, the hypothesis is rejected; a finite field with two weed sources does not guarantee full overgrowth unless the species exhibits an aggressive colonizing strategy, absent which a stable, patchy equilibrium is maintained.
