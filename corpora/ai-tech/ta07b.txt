India’s digital divide is shaped by a confluence of socioeconomic, geographic, and policy‑related factors that reinforce existing inequities. Rural regions, which house roughly 65 % of the population, suffer from inadequate broadband infrastructure, limited electricity reliability, and lower digital literacy, while urban centres benefit from higher income levels, better‑connected schools, and proactive private‑sector investment. Government initiatives such as BharatNet and the Digital India campaign aim to expand fibre‑optic connectivity and promote e‑governance, yet implementation bottlenecks — stemming from fragmented jurisdictional authority, financing gaps, and uneven enforcement — curtail their impact. Complementary approaches, including community‑led wireless networks, subsidised device schemes, and targeted skill‑development programmes, are essential to address both access and usage dimensions of the divide.
