Cellular automata, discrete dynamical systems defined on lattice grids of finite cells, have long intrigued researchers for their capacity to embody simple rule sets that generate complex spatial and temporal patterns; such emergent behavior mirrors core characteristics of biological replication and evolution, making them compelling analogues for modelling life‑like processes. This essay investigates whether elementary automata, particularly those exhibiting additive and self‑replicating configurations, can faithfully simulate the stochastic yet deterministic mechanisms underpinning organismal replication, mutation, and adaptive selection. By probing the boundary between algorithmic regularity and organic variability, the study aims to assess cellular automata not merely as computational curiosities but as viable frameworks for exploring evolutionary dynamics in silico.
