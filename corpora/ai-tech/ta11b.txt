An examination of the stopping time function for the 3n+1 process reveals a striking concentration of trajectories near the average logarithmic growth rate, while outliers exhibit markedly longer excursions before descending to 1. Statistical sampling of initial values up to 10^6 shows that the median stopping time scales approximately with log n, yet the tails follow a power‑law distribution, suggesting self‑similar fractal behavior in the sequence of iterates. Moreover, computational experiments indicate that parity patterns generate clusters of consecutive even steps, which accelerate reductions when preceded by a large odd term. These empirical trends support the conjecture’s plausibility, whilst also highlighting regions of irregular dynamics that merit further analytical scrutiny.
