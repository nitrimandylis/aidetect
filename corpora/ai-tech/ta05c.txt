In conclusion, the comparative analysis of iron‑rich dietary patterns across Tanzania reveals that traditional staples such as ugali, beans and leafy greens provide a modest but consistent source of dietary iron, whereas urban diets increasingly incorporate fortified cereals and red meat, yet often lack diversity due to economic constraints. Regional disparities, particularly between rural highlands and coastal lowlands, underscore the influence of agricultural practices and food availability on nutrient intake. These findings suggest that public‑health interventions should prioritize culturally tailored nutrition education and affordable fortification programs that complement existing food preferences, thereby enhancing iron status without imposing external dietary reforms. Future longitudinal studies are warranted to assess long‑term health outcomes.
