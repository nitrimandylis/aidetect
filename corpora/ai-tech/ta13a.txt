Rolling motion on an inclined plane is a classic illustration of the interplay between translational and rotational dynamics, yet real-world scenarios often involve insufficient friction that permits slipping. This research investigates how varying slip conditions alter the acceleration, angular velocity, and trajectory of a solid cylinder released from rest on an incline, aiming to elucidate the transition from pure rolling to mixed slipping. By quantifying the dependence of frictional coefficient, incline angle, and cylinder mass distribution on the kinematic evolution, the study connects theoretical mechanics with observable phenomena, offering insight into engineering applications such as vehicle traction control and sports equipment design.
