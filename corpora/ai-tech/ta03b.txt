Statistical analysis of histological data from 48 human retinas revealed a significant decline in cone density with advancing age, particularly in the parafoveal region where the loss averaged 0.8 % per decade (p < 0.01). Multivariate regression indicated that gender did not exert an independent effect on cone numbers, but interaction terms showed that older women experienced a marginally steeper decline than older men (β = −0.03, p = 0.04). Spatial mapping confirmed that rod degeneration preceded cone loss, yet the age‑related slope differed across retinal quadrants, being greatest temporally. These findings corroborate previous psychophysical reports of reduced colour discrimination in seniors and suggest that gender‑specific hormonal influences may modulate photoreceptor resilience, improving our understanding of age‑related visual decline.
