In this analysis the various two‑person zero‑sum poker models are examined through the lens of strategic equilibrium concepts. First, the classic normal‑form representation of poker is transformed into a matrix game, allowing the identification of mixed strategies that constitute Nash equilibria. Repeated interaction models introduce subgame perfection and trigger strategies, revealing how threat reputations alter betting frequencies. Bayesian games capture incomplete information about opponent hand strength, employing beliefs and Bayes’ updating to compute expected payoffs. Finally, stochastic approximations model noise in player actions, illustrating convergence toward stationary equilibrium distributions. Comparative statics show that increasing pot size or reducing bet limits shifts equilibrium allocations, offering insight into the comparative advantage of aggressive versus conservative play."
