In conclusion, while artificial neural networks and biological neural systems share functional parallels — such as information processing through interconnected units — their architectures diverge markedly in structure, dynamics, and development. ANNs rely on discrete layers, weighted connections, and static architectures optimized for specific tasks, whereas biological brains exhibit highly plastic, multi‑scale organizations that integrate chemical, electrical, and metabolic processes across variable time scales. Learning mechanisms also differ: ANNs typically employ explicit back‑propagation and gradient descent, while neural plasticity in vivo involves Hebbian modification, neuromodulation, and structural remodeling. Recognizing these distinctions underscores that ANNs are functional approximations rather than exact replicas, highlighting both the promise of bio‑inspired AI and the limits of purely algorithmic models.
