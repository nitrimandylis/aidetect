The economic structure of India constitutes the most significant determinant of its digital divide. With approximately sixty percent of the workforce engaged in agriculture and a substantial proportion of the population living below the poverty line, the resources required to purchase computing equipment simply do not exist for the majority of rural households. This economic reality means that infrastructural investment alone cannot bridge the divide; it must be accompanied by broader reform aimed at raising rural incomes. Consequently, any meaningful strategy must address the underlying economic conditions rather than merely the availability of technology.
