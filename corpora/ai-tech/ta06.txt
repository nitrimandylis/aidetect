Unequal access to digital infrastructure in India is fundamentally rooted in the economics of provision, where profitability dictates reach. Telecommunication firms, operating on market principles, prioritize investment in densely populated urban areas where high average revenue per user ensures a return on the capital-intensive deployment of fibre-optic networks and 4G/5G infrastructure. Conversely, rural regions, characterized by lower population density and reduced household incomes, present a weak business case, resulting in the provision of inferior or entirely absent connectivity. This supply-side logic is compounded by demand-side constraints; for low-income households, the upfront cost of a smartphone represents a significant proportion of annual earnings, while recurring data expenses compete with essential goods such as food and fuel.
