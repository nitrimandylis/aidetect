The implementation of RFID monitoring systems reveals a fundamental disparity in the perceived value of the technology between the two institutions, rooted in their core operational objectives. In hospitals, the benefits are predominantly clinical and logistical, where real-time location services for equipment and patient flow directly enhance efficiency and can indirectly improve patient safety by reducing time wasted on asset retrieval. This is a clear, quantifiable return on investment centered on resource management. Conversely, in schools, the justification often pivots towards administrative convenience and security, such as automating attendance and controlling access. However, this use case introduces a problematic dynamic; it transforms a pedagogical environment into a surveillance state, potentially eroding trust between students and staff.
