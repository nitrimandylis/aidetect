The polarimetry results for mixtures of glucose and fructose demonstrate the additive nature of optical rotation when measured at the sodium D-line (589 nm) under standardized conditions of 20 °C and a path length of 10 cm. Pure D-glucose exhibited a specific rotation of [α]₂₀ᴰ = +52.7°, while D-fructose showed a strongly negative rotation of [α]₂₀ᴰ = −92.4°, consistent with literature values. Mixtures containing 0.50 g of each sugar in 100 mL of deionized water produced net rotations that fell linearly between these two extremes, confirming that the observed angle is the arithmetic sum of the individual contributions from each monosaccharide. Slight deviations from strict linearity, of the order of ±0.3°, were attributed to instrumental uncertainty and minor temperature fluctuations during the 24-hour equilibration period, indicating that the method provides reliable quantitative data when appropriate controls are maintained.
