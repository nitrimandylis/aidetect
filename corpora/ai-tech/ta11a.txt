The 3n + 1 Conjecture, also known as the Collatz Conjecture, presents a deceptively simple iterative process: for any positive integer n, if n is even, divide it by two; if n is odd, multiply it by three and add one. Despite its elementary formulation, the conjecture that every starting value eventually reaches the cycle 4, 2, 1 has remained unproven for over eight decades, challenging the foundations of number theory and dynamical systems. This essay investigates a specific aspect of the conjecture’s complexity: the stopping time function, defined as the least number of iterations required for a given n to fall below its initial value. By computationally analysing the stopping times for large ranges of integers, this study aims to identify emergent patterns and potential bounds that may illuminate the underlying structure governing this enduring mathematical mystery.
