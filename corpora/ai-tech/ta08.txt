Artificial neural networks are typically organised into three functional layers, an architecture that reflects the flow of information through the system. The input layer receives the raw data, since no processing can occur in the absence of input. The hidden layer, which may itself comprise several sub-layers, performs the substantive computation as signals propagate through the network. Finally, the output layer returns the processed result. Each layer is composed of artificial neurons, commonly termed nodes, and it is the weighted connections between these nodes that enable the network to approximate the behaviour of its biological counterpart.
