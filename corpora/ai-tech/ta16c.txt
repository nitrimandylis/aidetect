In sum, this investigation demonstrates that cellular automata can indeed serve as a compelling framework for modeling replicating and evolving systems, provided that appropriate rule sets and boundary conditions are employed. The analysis of Wolfram’s elementary automata, coupled with extensions incorporating stochastic updates and multi‑state interactions, reveals emergent behaviors reminiscent of mutation, selection, and speciation without explicit genetic machinery. Moreover, quantitative metrics derived from information entropy and pattern complexity substantiate the parallel between computational dynamics and biological evolution. While limitations remain — such as oversimplified state spaces and the absence of biochemical realism — the findings suggest that cellular automata offer a valuable, experimentally tractable platform for exploring fundamental principles of emergence and adaptation.
