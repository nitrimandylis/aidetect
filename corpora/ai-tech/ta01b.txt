The data reveal a clear phase-dependent response to the one-hour light pulse, with the magnitude and direction of the shift varying systematically across the circadian cycle. When administered during the early subjective night (CT13-CT15), the pulse produced significant phase delays in locomotor activity onset, with a mean shift of -2.8 hours (±0.4). Conversely, pulses delivered in the late subjective night (CT19-CT21) yielded phase advances of approximately +1.9 hours (±0.5). Notably, light exposure during the subjective day (CT5-CT7) elicited no measurable phase shift, consistent with the established dead zone in the phase response curve. These results confirm that a relatively brief, one-hour stimulus is sufficient to robustly modulate circadian phase, though the effect is contingent upon the precise timing of administration.
