In conclusion, the investigation of Alhazen’s billiard problem illuminates the profound interplay between geometric intuition and analytic rigor in medieval optics, revealing how a seemingly simple reflection question catalyzed foundational advances in calculus and analytical geometry. By reconstructing Alhazen’s experimental methodology and translating his geometric constraints into modern coordinate terms, the study demonstrates the enduring validity of his iterative approach while highlighting the limitations imposed by the limitations of medieval mathematical notation and conceptual frameworks. The findings underscore the necessity of interdisciplinary scholarship in revisiting historical texts, suggesting that further exploration of medieval problem‑solving strategies could enrich contemporary understandings of both the history of science and the evolution of analytical techniques.
