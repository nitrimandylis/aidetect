One of the most enduring problems to emerge from the classical study of optics and geometry is the billiard problem attributed to the medieval polymath Ibn al-Haytham, known in the West as Alhazen. The problem, in its original formulation, asks for the point on a circular mirror at which a ray of light from a given source will reflect to a given observer, a question rooted in Alhazen’s critique of Ptolemaic optics. Over centuries, this challenge has transcended its optical origins to become a celebrated locus in geometry, as it cannot be solved using only a straightedge and compass for arbitrary positions. This essay will trace the transformation of Alhazen’s Billiard Problem from a practical inquiry in catoptrics into a foundational example in algebraic geometry, examining its mathematical significance and the evolution of the methods required to solve it.
