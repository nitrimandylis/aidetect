Esters such as ethyl aminobenzoate (benzocaine) are commonly prepared by Fischer esterification of p‑aminobenzoic acid with ethanol under acidic conditions, and the reaction’s outcome depends strongly on the duration of reflux. Extended reflux times can drive the equilibrium toward product formation, potentially increasing yield, yet they may also promote side reactions or hydrolysis that affect purity. Understanding this relationship is essential for optimizing laboratory protocols that balance high yield with pharmaceutical‑grade purity. Accordingly, the investigation asks: how does reflux time influence both the quantitative yield and the analytical purity of benzocaine produced by reflux, and to what extent does varying reflux duration improve the overall efficacy of the synthesis.
