The digital divide in India persists as a multifaceted challenge shaped by intersecting socioeconomic, infrastructural, and demographic factors, including income inequality, rural-urban disparities, gender gaps, and varying levels of digital literacy. While government initiatives such as Digital India, BharatNet, and PMGDISHA have accelerated infrastructure rollout and basic skills training, their impact remains uneven due to implementation bottlenecks, affordability barriers, and low meaningful usage among marginalized groups. Bridging this divide demands a holistic strategy that integrates affordable device access, localized and multilingual content, robust public-private partnerships, and community-led digital literacy programs. Ultimately, closing the gap is not merely a technical objective but a prerequisite for equitable participation in India’s digital economy, requiring sustained political will and adaptive policy frameworks that prioritize the most excluded populations.
