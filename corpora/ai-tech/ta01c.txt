In conclusion, this investigation demonstrates that one-hour light pulses applied during the subjective night can indeed shift the phase of circadian locomotor rhythms, with the magnitude and direction of the shift depending critically on the timing of the pulse relative to the endogenous cycle. Pulses delivered in the early subjective night produced consistent phase delays, whereas those applied toward the late subjective night induced phase advances, consistent with established phase response curves. These findings support the hypothesis that the circadian pacemaker remains acutely sensitive to light even during the dark period, reinforcing the entrainment model of circadian organization. However, limitations including small sample size and inter-individual variability suggest caution in generalizing the results.
