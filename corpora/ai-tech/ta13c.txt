This investigation has demonstrated that slipping profoundly alters the motion of a cylinder on an incline, transitioning its behavior from the predictable, energy-conserving model of pure rolling to a more complex dynamic governed by kinetic friction. The experimental results confirmed the theoretical prediction that the linear acceleration of a slipping cylinder decreases, while its angular acceleration increases, relative to the no-slip case. While the derived model successfully predicted the onset of the slip angle, discrepancies in the measured accelerations, particularly at steeper angles, highlight the limitations of a simplified Coulomb friction model and suggest factors such as surface inconsistencies and a velocity-dependent friction coefficient may be significant. Ultimately, this study underscores the necessity of incorporating slip to accurately model real-world rolling systems.
