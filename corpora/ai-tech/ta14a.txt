Mountain biking has evolved into a high‑velocity sport where riders constantly push the limits of equipment performance, yet the factors that constrain maximum deceleration remain inadequately quantified. This investigation seeks to identify and assess the primary limitations—ranging from tire‑track interaction and brake system mechanics to rider posture and terrain compliance—that restrict achievable braking forces on a freeride bicycle. By analysing empirical data from laboratory tests and on‑site field measurements, the study will elucidate how material properties, hydraulic actuation, and dynamic load transfer collectively define a theoretical braking ceiling for such bikes. Understanding these constraints not only enhances safety considerations but also informs design refinements for future off‑road bicycles.
