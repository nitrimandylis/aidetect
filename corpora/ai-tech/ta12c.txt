Overall, this study demonstrates that applying rigorous game‑theoretic frameworks to variant forms of two‑person zero‑sum poker reveals nuanced strategy spaces that extend beyond traditional equilibrium analysis. By quantifying the impact of differing betting structures, stack depths, and payoff modifications, the models highlight how optimal bluffing frequencies and hand‑selection thresholds shift under alternative rule sets, offering fresh insights into the universal dynamics of conflict and cooperation in restricted‑information games. These results affirm the versatility of zero‑sum theory in capturing complex behavioural patterns and suggest avenues for further research, such as integrating learning algorithms or exploring multi‑agent extensions to deepen our understanding of strategic decision‑making. Future simulations could also test adaptive opponent models to assess robustness.
