In sum, while Shor’s algorithm demonstrates that a sufficiently large, error‑corrected quantum computer could factor integers in polynomial time and thus break RSA and ECC, the practical realization of such a machine remains distant. Current quantum hardware is limited by decoherence, gate fidelity, and scalability, and the overhead required for fault‑tolerant operation far exceeds present capabilities. Consequently, most widely deployed cryptosystems retain sufficient security for the foreseeable future, though hybrid approaches and post‑quantum candidates are being explored. The transition will likely be driven more by advances in classical algorithms and policy considerations than by imminent quantum breakthroughs, underscoring that quantum threat is a long‑term risk rather than an immediate vulnerability.
