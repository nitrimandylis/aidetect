In order to determine the specific rotation of an optically active substance, three quantities must be measured: the observed rotation of plane-polarised light, the path length of the polarimeter tube, and the concentration of the solution. Specific rotation is formally defined as the rotation produced by a solution of unit concentration in a tube of ten centimetres, measured at a specified wavelength and temperature. Because this value is characteristic of a given compound under standardised conditions, it serves as a reliable means of identification and provides the theoretical foundation upon which the subsequent experimental investigation is built.
