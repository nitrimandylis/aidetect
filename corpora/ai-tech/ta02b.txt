Statistical analysis reveals a moderately strong positive correlation (r = 0.62, p < 0.01) between the density of Pandanus heliacorpus individuals per hectare and the observed abundance of Macrobrachium shrimp across the surveyed streams, suggesting that the structural complexity of P. heliacorpus stands contributes to habitat suitability for the crustaceans. Regression modelling indicates that each additional 10 % increase in Pandanus cover predicts an approximate 8 % rise in Macrobrachium catch per unit effort, even after controlling for water depth and substrate type. However, variation in shrimp size distribution and seasonal migrations introduce residual heterogeneity, highlighting that while Pandanus density is a significant predictor, it operates within a broader ecological context of abiotic and biotic factors.
