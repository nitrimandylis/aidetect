In recent years, rising prevalence of anemia alongside widespread dietary shifts has prompted a critical examination of iron consumption patterns throughout Tanzania's diverse regions. This study investigates how traditional staples, fortified foods, and market‑derived provisions contribute to daily iron intake, comparing urban centers such as Dar es Salaam with rural districts like Iringa and Mwanza. By integrating household survey data, national nutrition surveys, and ethnographic observations, the research aims to identify gaps between recommended dietary allowances and actual consumption across socioeconomic groups. Understanding these disparities will illuminate public health implications, guide targeted interventions, and shed light on how cultural practices and agricultural policies shape iron nutrition in contemporary Tanzanian society.
