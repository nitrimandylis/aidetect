Artificial neural networks (ANNs) have transformed machine learning by mimicking the computational principles of biological brains, yet their architectures diverge sharply from the intricate structures found in nature. This essay investigates the parallels and contrasts between the layered, modular designs of contemporary ANNs and the hierarchical, recurrent, and parallel-processing networks of biological neurons. By examining factors such as connectivity patterns, activation mechanisms, learning dynamics, and evolutionary constraints, the analysis aims to clarify how design choices in artificial systems reflect both functional imperatives and emergent biological properties. Understanding these parallels not only illuminates the underlying science of information processing but also informs the development of more biologically inspired artificial intelligence.
