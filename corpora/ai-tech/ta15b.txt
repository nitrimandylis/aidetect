In the analysis, the structural disparities between artificial and biological neural networks become evident through their distinct organizational principles. Artificial networks employ layered, feed‑forward or recurrent topologies with fixed connections and discrete activation functions, whereas biological systems exhibit highly interconnected, layered yet incessantly rewiring neuronal circuits supported by synaptic plasticity. The parameter regimes also differ: ANNs rely on millions of high‑precision floating‑point weights optimized by gradient‑based algorithms, while brains leverage sparse, analog synaptic strengths modulated by neuromodulators and experience‑dependent remodeling. Energy consumption likewise diverges, with ANNs demanding substantial computational power for inference, contrasting sharply with the brain’s efficient, parallel processing that operates within limited metabolic budgets.
