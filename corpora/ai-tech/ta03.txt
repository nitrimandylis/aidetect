The 3n+1 conjecture, commonly known as the Collatz conjecture, occupies a curious position in mathematics: its statement is elementary enough for a schoolchild to understand, yet it has resisted proof for the better part of a century. The conjecture is generally attributed to Lothar Collatz, who is believed to have formulated it in the 1930s, although its precise origins remain uncertain because it circulated informally within the mathematical community for decades. This combination of simplicity and intractability has made it a compelling object of study, and it continues to attract significant attention from both amateur and professional mathematicians alike.
