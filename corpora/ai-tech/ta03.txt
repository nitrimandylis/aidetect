The 3n+1 conjecture, first proposed by Lothar Collatz in 1937, asserts that iterative application of T(n)=n/2 for even n and T(n)=3n+1 for odd n will eventually reach 1 for all positive integers. In this essay the stopping time function σ(n) is defined as the smallest k such that T^k(n) ≤ n and the total stopping time as the first k with T^k(n)=1, assuming the conjecture holds. Empirical computation for n ≤ 10^7 reveals that σ(n) grows irregularly but exhibits statistically regular behaviour, with average values increasing logarithmically and occasional large spikes corresponding to numbers with long trajectories.
