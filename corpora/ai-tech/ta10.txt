The remarkable capacity of cellular automata to generate complexity from simple rules invites a natural question: can such systems be used to model genuinely biological processes, specifically self-replication and evolution? Investigating this question requires examining several distinct rule sets in order to assess the feasibility of such a simulation. Trivial replicators, in which a cell merely converts its neighbours without exhibiting any more sophisticated behaviour, are excluded from consideration, as are asynchronous automata that lack a shared clock. The investigation therefore focuses on those rule sets that demonstrate genuinely emergent complexity.
