Cellular automata (CA) offer a mathematically tractable yet computationally efficient framework in which replication and Darwinian evolution can be simulated through simple local rules acting upon a discrete lattice. Conway’s Game of Life demonstrates how birth, persistence and death of patterns emerge purely from neighbourhood interactions, and self-replicating structures analogous to von Neumann’s universal constructors have been identified within related rule sets. When replication is coupled with heritable mutation of either genotype or phenotype—as exemplified by platforms such as Avida, Tierra or iterated CA models—populations of digital “organisms” undergo competitive selection, genetic drift and adaptation. Small perturbations to the underlying rules can generate macro-evolutionary phenomena including speciation, extinction and the emergence of multicellularity, thereby permitting rigorous investigation of evolutionary dynamics, complexity and open-ended innovation within controlled artificial ecosystems.
