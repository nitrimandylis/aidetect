Shor's algorithm demonstrates that a sufficiently large, error‑corrected quantum computer can factor integers and compute discrete logarithms in polynomial time, directly threatening the security foundations of RSA, Diffie‑Hellman, and elliptic‑curve cryptography, which rely on the assumed hardness of these problems. While present‑day quantum hardware remains noisy and limited to fewer than a few hundred qubits, theoretical analyses indicate that scaling to the thousands of logical qubits required for breaking 2048‑bit RSA would demand advances in qubit coherence, error‑correction overhead, and algorithmic optimisation that are not yet realised. Consequently, current cryptographic schemes are not immediately obsolete, but the prospect of quantum‑enabled attacks necessitates a proactive transition to post‑quantum algorithms.
