The human retina contains two principal types of photoreceptor cells—rods and cones—responsible for scotopic vision and high‑acuity color perception, respectively. While extensive histological and psychophysical studies have characterized the distribution, density, and functional properties of these cells in younger populations, comparatively few investigations have examined how natural aging processes and biological sex influence their structural integrity and visual performance. Understanding whether age‑related degeneration or gender‑specific hormonal factors differently alter rod and cone morphology, sensitivity, and susceptibility to disease may illuminate underlying mechanisms of visual decline and inform targeted therapeutic strategies. This investigation therefore seeks to systematically assess the correlations between chronological age, biological sex, and quantitative measures of photoreceptor cell density and function.
