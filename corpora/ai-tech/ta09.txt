In this operation, N denotes the integer to be factorised, while X represents a randomly chosen integer greater than one and smaller than N. The modulo operation returns the remainder of the division, and the random integer is raised to the power held in the first register before being divided by N, with the resulting value stored in the second register. Because the first register exists in a superposition of states, the result is likewise a superposition encompassing every possible outcome simultaneously. This property constitutes the fundamental advantage that quantum computation holds over classical approaches to factorisation.
