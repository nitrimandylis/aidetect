While classical computers rely on the computational difficulty of factoring large semiprime numbers to secure systems like RSA, Shor’s algorithm demonstrates that a sufficiently powerful quantum computer could solve this problem in polynomial time, thereby threatening the mathematical foundations of current asymmetric cryptography. By exploiting quantum superposition and entanglement, the algorithm efficiently determines the period of a function, which in turn enables the extraction of prime factors and the subsequent derivation of private keys. Although contemporary quantum hardware remains too error-prone and limited in qubit count to execute Shor’s algorithm against industrially deployed key sizes, the theoretical vulnerability is absolute; once fault-tolerant quantum computers achieve sufficient scale, widely used public-key systems would become computationally obsolete, compelling the urgent transition to post-quantum cryptographic standards.
