In summary, the analysis of retinal photoreceptor density across age and gender reveals a consistent decline in cone and rod counts with advancing age, while gender‑related variations show modest but statistically significant differences, with males exhibiting slightly higher cone : rod ratios in the foveal region and females displaying marginally greater peripheral rod density. These findings, corroborated by histological and electrophysiological data, suggest that physiological maturation processes and hormonal influences modulate photoreceptor maintenance and susceptibility to degeneration. Consequently, the study supports the hypothesis that age exerts a dominant effect on photoreceptor health, whereas gender contributes as a secondary variable that must be accounted for in clinical assessments and future therapeutic strategies targeting retinal disorders."
