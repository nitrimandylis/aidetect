The experimental data ultimately demonstrate a clear, linear relationship between the observed optical rotation of a mixed solution and the relative concentrations of (+)-glucose and (–)-fructose. As the proportion of fructose increased, the measured rotation decreased proportionally, transitioning from positive through zero to negative values, precisely as predicted by the additive nature of specific rotations. The calculated specific rotation of the mixtures aligned closely with theoretical values derived from the pure enantiopure solutions, confirming the validity of the linear model. Minor deviations likely resulted from temperature fluctuations and the mutarotation of glucose. Consequently, polarimetry offers a reliable method for determining the composition of such binary sugar mixtures, underscoring the broader applicability of optical activity in quantitative analysis.
