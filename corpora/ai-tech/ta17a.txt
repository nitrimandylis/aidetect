The advent of quantum computing presents a profound challenge to the cryptographic foundations that secure contemporary digital infrastructure. Classical systems such as RSA and ECC rely on the computational intractability of integer factorization and the discrete logarithm problem, assumptions that hold against conventional processors. However, the development of Shor’s algorithm, which solves both problems in polynomial time on a sufficiently powerful quantum computer, fundamentally undermines this security paradigm. This essay investigates the extent to which quantum computers, through the practical implementation of Shor’s work, render current public-key cryptographic methods obsolete. By evaluating theoretical proofs, the quantum error-correction challenges involved in scaling hardware, and the emergence of post-quantum alternatives, the analysis assesses whether this threat is imminent or remains a theoretical horizon requiring strategic preparation.
