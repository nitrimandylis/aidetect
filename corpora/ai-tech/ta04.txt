In two-person zero-sum poker, the game can be formally modelled using the formalism of extensive-form games with imperfect information, where chance nodes represent stochastic card dealing and player nodes encode decision choices of betting, checking, or folding under conditions of asymmetric knowledge. Applying the minimax theorem to such models demonstrates that every finite zero-sum poker variant possesses a value and that optimal strategies exist, though these strategies are necessarily mixed because deterministic play would be exploitable by a rational opponent. Furthermore, techniques such as sequence-form linear programming allow the computation of equilibrium strategies in abstractions of the full game, revealing how betting structures, stack depths, and the ratio of bluffing to value hands shape equilibrium frequencies and the long-run expected payoff for each player.
