Analyzing national dietary surveys and household food consumption reports reveals a stark disparity in iron intake between urban coastal populations and rural highland communities across Tanzania. While urban centers exhibit higher consumption of animal-source foods such as meat and legumes, contributing marginally to daily iron adequacy, rural diets are dominated by staple grains—maize, cassava, and millet—whose non‑heme iron bioavailability is limited by phytates and low vitamin C accompaniment. Consequently, the prevalence of anemia among women of reproductive age escalates markedly in the highlands, whereas coastal metropolises display modest but still insufficient iron sufficiency rates. These divergent patterns underscore the necessity for region‑specific nutritional interventions that address both food composition and cultural dietary practices.
