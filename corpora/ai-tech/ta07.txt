Mechanical efficiency provides the theoretical foundation for this investigation, since a bow functions essentially as a spring that stores and releases elastic potential energy. Efficiency is defined as the ratio of useful work output to work input, conventionally expressed as a percentage, and the law of conservation of energy ensures that this value can never exceed one hundred percent. In the context of archery, the useful output is the kinetic energy imparted to the arrow, while the input is the work done in drawing the bow. This definition therefore necessitates two distinct experimental measurements.
