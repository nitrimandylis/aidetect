Efficiency was calculated as the ratio of arrow kinetic energy, derived from chronograph velocity and arrow mass, to the work done in drawing the bow, obtained by integrating the force-draw curve. As draw length increased from 24 cm to 32 cm in 2 cm increments, stored energy rose quadratically with draw weight, while measured arrow speed increased at a diminishing rate due to increased string stretch and limb hysteresis. Consequently, bow efficiency rose from approximately 62% at 24 cm to a peak of 71% at 30 cm before declining slightly to 69% at 32 cm. The initial improvement reflects better energy transfer, whereas the subsequent decline indicates increased internal losses and non-linear limb deformation at extended draw.
