Game theory, the mathematical study of strategic interaction, provides a powerful framework for dissecting the complex decision‑making processes inherent in poker, especially when the game is abstracted into two‑person zero‑sum settings. By modeling each hand as a deterministic payoff matrix and treating players’ mixed strategies as probability distributions over possible actions, the analysis isolates the essential tension between exploitation and uncertainty. This introduction outlines the transition from classical optimal‑play solutions to contemporary extensions that incorporate incomplete information, bluffing, and tournament dynamics, thereby establishing a unified theoretical platform. The subsequent sections will examine several representative models — such as the hawk‑dove, Cournot‑style betting, and Bayesian Nash formulations — and assess how their equilibrium predictions illuminate optimal betting and folding behaviors."
