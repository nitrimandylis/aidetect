Crucially, the data indicates that the relationship between reflux duration and yield is not positively linear but rather follows an optimum curve, peaking at the 45-minute mark. Extending the reflux beyond this point led to a marked decrease in percentage yield, likely due to the hydrolysis of the ester product back into its constituent acid and alcohol under the prolonged acidic aqueous conditions. This assertion is supported by the purity analysis; although all samples yielded relatively high purity, thin-layer chromatography and melting point determination revealed a slight increase in impurities, potentially unreacted 4-aminobenzoic acid, in the samples refluxed for 60 and 75 minutes. This suggests that while longer reflux may drive the initial esterification forward, it simultaneously promotes the reverse reaction, ultimately compromising both the quantity and quality of the final benzocaine product.
