The mangrove ecosystem of Southeast Asia provides a dynamic interface where terrestrial flora and aquatic fauna interact, influencing each other's demographic patterns. Pandanus heliacorpus, a halophytic shrub that stabilizes substrate and offers refuge, exhibits variable densities across tidal gradients, potentially shaping habitat availability for associated invertebrates. Macrobrachium spp., opportunistic freshwater‑brackish shrimp, rely on complex root structures for feeding, breeding, and predator avoidance, making their abundance sensitive to changes in vegetative cover. Understanding how the spatial distribution and density of Pandanus heliacorpus correlate with the population size of Macrobrachium could elucidate facilitative or competitive mechanisms within this ecotone, informing conservation strategies that address both plant and faunal components of mangrove resilience.
