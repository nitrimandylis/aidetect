Alhazen's billiard problem asks where a ball must strike the cushion of a circular table in order to rebound towards a second ball, and its solution illustrates the deep connection between optics and geometry. The law of reflection requires that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal, which in the circular case is the radius drawn to the point of contact. Consequently the problem can be restated as the construction of an isosceles triangle inscribed in the circle whose equal sides pass through the two given points, transforming a physical question into a purely geometrical one.
