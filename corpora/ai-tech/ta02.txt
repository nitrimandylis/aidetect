Alhazen's billiard problem, formulated by Ibn al-Haytham in his *Book of Optics* (c. 1021), asks for the point on a circular mirror where a ray of light from a given source must strike to reach a specified observer after reflection. Geometrically, this reduces to finding a point P on a given circle such that the lines from two fixed interior points A and B to P make equal angles with the radius at P—a condition equivalent to the equality of angles of incidence and reflection. Alhazen solved this using intersecting conic sections, though his method was synthetic and cumbersome. Analytic geometry later revealed the problem leads to a quartic equation, explaining its resistance to straightedge-and-compass construction.
