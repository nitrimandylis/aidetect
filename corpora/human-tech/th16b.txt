The one-dimensional two-state "elementary" automata featured by Stephen Wolfram offer an interesting jumping-off point into the world of simple programs generating complex results. In this simulation, the "world" consists of a one-dimensional line, in which the state of a cell is determined by the states of its neighbors and itself from the previous tick. Since there are relatively few possible rule sets in such a setting, each possible rule set is assigned a number from zero to two hundred and fifty. five.
