The law of reflection states that the angle of reflection and angle of incidence are equal, with each angle being measured from the normal to the boundary. In figure 1, the incident path must have an angle equal to the reflected path. The boundary in our case would be a tangent line drawn to the point on the border of the circle where the ball A bounces off the circular side to ball B. Another way to express this problem is, "to describe in a given circle an isosceles triangle whose legs pass through two given points made inside the circle". This is useful because it allows us to relate the billiard balls to chords within the circle.
