There are two ways in which the maxim could be shown to be impossible to will from a perfectly rational being. One must either show that someone's life is worth preserving unconditionally (as Kant seems to argue implicitly) or one must show that taking one's life because of more suffering than pleasure is irrational. The latter route seems to hold more promise. As a rational agent one is likely to recognize the intrinsic worth of a will. Kant himself places a lot of emphasis on the worth of one's will stating that one should never violate the autonomy of another rational being. An interesting point arises here. If one is to never treat a will as a means to an end, then perhaps to take one's own life is to violate one's own autonomous will. Kant makes it explicit with his Formula 18 emp, p. 251.
