In contrast with Baudouin, Lumumba looks explicitly at us. And we get more involved in this mysterious figure. He has a remarkably Western look; suit and glasses in the fifties fashion, even his face is painted slightly more Caucasian. His demeanour is remarkably self-assured yet friendly contrasting with the insecure Baudouin. It suggests that Tuymans wants to confront us with the hypothesis of the black intellectual' - a sheer impossibility in the collective memory of the white man, where the African black people were believed to be disadvantaged savages, uneducated and below the 'White race'.
