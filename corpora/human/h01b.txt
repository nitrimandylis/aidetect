A strong contrast exists between two of the book's main relationships, accentuated by the settings in which they develop. Emma gradually comes to realise her feelings for Mr Knightley, a rational kind of attachment, based on longstanding affection, and formed in the peaceful countryside, whereas Jane Fairfax and Frank Churchill fall passionately in love at the seaside, in Weymouth, which I shall argue has morally dubious connotations.
