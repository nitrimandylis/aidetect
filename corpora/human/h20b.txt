LeVay does not see the brain as a sexual orientation organ, but, rather, as an important part of the neural pathway engaged in sexual behavior, meaning that the brain's anatomy influences sexual behavior. He later confirmed his theory when he discovered a similar difference between the six to 10 percent of male sheep that display same-sex attractions, and the 90+ percent attracted to females. A section of the corpus callosum that is one-third larger in homosexual men than in heterosexuals or females, discovered by Laura Allen and Roger Gorski, also supported LeVay's theory. This discovery states, "Homosexual men are more likely to have female-typical neuroanatomy than heterosexual men" (Myers, 2003).
