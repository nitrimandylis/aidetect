Tydeman has suggested that Faustus can be viewed as 'a spiritual pioneer, seeking to clear away the obstacles placed in the way of man's progress by a repressive god.' Source: Tydeman, p. 12. He also notes that George Santayana has called Faustus 'a martyr to everything that the renaissance prized—power, curious knowledge, enterprise, wealth and beauty.' Source: Tydeman, p. 17.
