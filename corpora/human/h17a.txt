Worst of all was the handling of the talks and their outcome. Disastrously, when the talks at Camp David reached stalemate, Clinton and Barak went public vilifying Arafat and blaming him exclusively for the failure of the talks, while Barak was warmly praised for his great statesmanship and the great offer he had made. Its main immediate effect was to totally undermine Palestinian trust in Clinton as an impartial mediator. Almost equally disastrous, rather than agreeing to continue dialogue behind the scenes to try to narrow the gaps on the various key issues and to resume negotiations at a later date, Clinton and Barak called peace negotiations off completely. Barak went back home, announcing, firstly that he would 'take time out of the peace process' and, secondly, that he would hold new elections in February 2001 - elections, ironically, he had little chance of winning without a peace deal.
