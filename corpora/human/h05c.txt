Again, the Elves' innate femininity is highlighted by their affinity with nature. Tolkien's letters reveal his love of the natural environment and aversion to industrialisations, a sentiment reflected in Saruman's 'treason' - felling the trees of Isengard to fuel the fires of his smithies. Feminine races such as Hobbits and Elves live in natural settings, whereas masculine races such as Orcs, Men and Dwarves live in cities or underground. Whilst these places are centres for mining and construction, Elvish havens such as Rivendell and Lothlorien are devoted to song and art, and are relief to weary travellers. Frodo's memories of Rivendell highlight this, Merely to be there was a cure for weariness, fear and sadness' (p219). His anecdotes suggest Rivendell to be like a womb - a temporary, almost maternally-comforting refuge from the hardship of life.
