Here, Malouf's abstract tone allows him to explicitly articulate his own creative process The act of imagination that Malouf as well as The Child engages in, and that Ovid and the reader are learning, is one that uses sound not to communicate but to transform. In this quote, Malouf draws attention to the twofold character of words: as pure sound and as signifiers, and he deliberately privileges the first. Seamus Heaney develops this idea further when he discusses T.S. Eliot's notion of the auditory imagination, which is a way of 'thinking of the relationship between the word as pure vocable, as articulate noise, and the word as etymological occurrence, as symptom of human history, memory and attachments. 15 Malouf sets up this dichotomy between sound and meaning only to reconfigure it.
