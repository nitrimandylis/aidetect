In The Groundwork Kant puts forth the following maxim: "From self-love I make it my principle to shorten my life if its continuance threatens more evil than it promises pleasure" This is the maxim Kant uses as an example of a subjective principle that is contrary to perfect duty, as he claims that it "cannot possibly hold as a universal law of nature" (i.e. it cannot be applied universally). Kant claims that a contradiction arises because the purpose of self-love is to preserve life, and yet in the above maxim self-love is used as a reason to end life. The concept of self-love cannot both further and destroy life. In order to critically analyze Kant's argument, it is imperative to examine the concepts of "self-love" and "universal law of nature" in more depth.
