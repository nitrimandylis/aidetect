In another example, the formation of Catholic, Muslim, or Jewish schools simultaneously exhibits the celebration of culture as called for by multiculturalism, but also segregation for it both celebrates a particular culture, but to the exclusion of others (especially in private schools, or in nations where Catholic education is private, and therefore not obligated to include other cultures). Indeed, radical multiculturalism seeks to have absolute acceptance, in a way forming a community built upon very distinct groups of peoples. But even here there is contradiction as Derrida himself states: If by community one implies [...] a fundamental agreement beneath the phenomena of discord or war, then I don't believe in it very much and I sense in it as much threat as promise.
