To investigate the motion of a cylinder rolling down an inclined plane, an ultrasonic motion detector was used to record the position of the cylinder as a function of time. The detector operates by emitting pulses of ultrasound and measuring the interval before the reflected pulse returns, from which the distance to the moving object is continuously calculated and logged. This arrangement offers the advantage of collecting a dense series of position measurements without physical contact, thereby avoiding the frictional interference that mechanical timing methods might introduce. From the recorded position-time data the velocity and acceleration of the cylinder can be obtained by successive differentiation, allowing the constant acceleration predicted for a body rolling without slipping to be tested directly. The measured value can then be compared with the theoretical acceleration derived from the equations of rotational and translational motion, in which the moment of inertia of the cylinder plays a decisive role.
